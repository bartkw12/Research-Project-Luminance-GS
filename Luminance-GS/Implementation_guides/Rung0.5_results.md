# Rung 0.5 results: what the "detach" gain is made of

Measurement only, no new method. Rung 0 found that detaching the pseudo-target (`--detach_pseudo_target`) raised brightness-matched PSNR by about 2 dB on chair, sofa and bike. That flag did two things at once:

- it stopped the enhancement loss from training the **curve**;
- it froze the per-view **colour basis** (the paper's M_k) at RGB, because the basis reaches the loss only through the pseudo-target.

Rung 0.5 separates the two with a 2 × 2 design. That needed 30 new runs: 5 LOM scenes × {`frozenbasis`, `detachcurve`} × seeds 0–2, 10k steps each.

| | Colour basis learned | Colour basis frozen at RGB |
|---|---|---|
| **Curve trained by the enhancement loss** | `baseline` (official) | `frozenbasis` |
| **Curve trained by the priors only** | `detachcurve` | `detach` |

All 60 runs were then re-scored from their checkpoints with three additions:
- colour metrics;
- consistency measured against one shared reference depth per scene;
- a zero-basis counterfactual: the run's own curve applied with the basis reset to RGB.

Outputs are in [`summary_rung05/`](../results/rung0/summary_rung05/) ([`tables.md`](../results/rung0/summary_rung05/tables.md)), regenerated by `python rung05_analyze.py`. *Measured* means read from the runs; *inferred* marks an interpretation. "Real" means larger than 2× the per-run seed std, with 3 seeds per cell.

## Answers

**Q1. Was the confound real, and how far did the baseline colour basis rotate?**

The confound was real.
- In all 15 detach runs, every view's basis parameters are exactly 0. In all 15 frozenbasis runs, the end-of-training assertion confirmed the same.
- The learned basis barely rotates: mean 0.02° (sofa) to 0.49° (bike), worst view 0.79° (buu).

Small as it is, the rotation matters. Resetting a baseline run's basis to RGB, while keeping its curve, improves its pseudo-targets in every scene:
- brightness-matched PSNR +0.16 (sofa) to +0.93 dB (buu);
- hue error −0.5° (sofa) to −15° (shrub: 23.3° → 8.3°).

Across views, the larger the rotation, the larger the improvement. Spearman ρ between a view's rotation and its improvement (hue error or brightness-matched PSNR) is 0.6–0.8 in buu, 0.2–0.5 in chair and shrub, and 0.1–0.4 in bike. On bike the reset mostly removes a reddish cast over the whole image ([zero-basis figure](../results/rung0/summary_rung05/figures/zero_basis_bike_29.png)).
- *Inferred:* the curve is steep at the dark end, so tiny changes to the curve's input are amplified.
- Caveat: this counterfactual reuses a curve trained alongside the rotated basis. It is not a retrained run.

**Q2. How much of the detach gain comes from freezing the colour basis?**

Little, and the answer depends on which effect is counted first.

| Scene | Detach gain, brightness-matched PSNR | Basis share, frozen first | Basis share, frozen last |
|---|---|---|---|
| chair | +2.15 dB | 26% | 15% |
| sofa | +2.19 dB | 31% | −29% |
| bike | +1.87 dB | −23% | 0% |
| pooled | +1.26 dB | 42% | 10% |

- "Frozen first" means comparing frozenbasis with baseline; "frozen last" means comparing detach with detachcurve.
- For LPIPS the pooled share is 64% (frozen first) and 11% (frozen last). On bike, where LPIPS falls by 0.118, the share is 0% either way.

The two orders disagree because freezing the basis only helps while the curve is still attached. Once the curve is detached, the learned basis hardly moves (bike 0.000°, chair 0.012°, sofa 0.006°), so there is little left to freeze. The averaged basis effect is real in only two cases: buu (+0.71 dB, 2.0×) and chair (+0.43 dB, 2.3×).

The basis does improve one thing consistently: colour angle error drops in buu, chair, sofa and bike (by 0.16°–0.50°, 2.2–7×). Freezing it alone also gives the best raw PSNR pooled over scenes (19.40 vs 18.47 dB). It is the best cell overall on buu: brightness-matched PSNR 27.11 vs 26.00, LPIPS 0.166 vs 0.186 ([figure](../results/rung0/summary_rung05/figures/val_metrics_per_cell.png)).

**Q3. How much comes from stopping the enhancement loss from training the curve?**

Most of it. The curve effect on brightness-matched PSNR, and the share of the detach gain when the curve is cut first:

| Scene | Curve effect | Share, curve first |
|---|---|---|
| chair | +1.72 dB (9×) | 85% |
| sofa | +2.16 dB (5.7×) | 129% |
| bike | +2.08 dB (27×) | 100% |
| pooled | +0.93 dB (5.1×) | 90% |

The whole bike LPIPS gain (−0.118, 10×) is the curve effect. Bike's `detachcurve` and `detach` cells are indistinguishable on every metric.

The exception is buu, where detaching the curve *hurts*: LPIPS +0.026 (4.5×). Shrub shows no real effect of either factor; its seed noise is 0.75 dB.

**Q4. Is there an interaction?**

Yes. Pooled, the two effects are sub-additive. On brightness-matched PSNR:
- freezing the basis alone gives +0.52 dB;
- detaching the curve alone gives +1.13 dB;
- `detach`, which does both, gives +1.26 dB, not +1.65 dB.

The pooled interaction is −0.40 dB (2.2×). By scene:
- **Sofa** has the strongest interaction (−1.32 dB, 3.4×). Its best cell is `detachcurve` (28.04 dB, vs detach 27.41), but that edge is only 1.6× noise.
- **Bike** goes the other way (+0.43 dB, 5.6×). Freezing the basis slightly hurts while the curve is attached (−0.43 dB) and changes nothing once it is detached.
- **LPIPS** shows real interactions in buu (+0.023) and sofa (+0.041).

**Q5. Does freezing the basis remove the hue flips?**

**No.** In bike view 29 (seed 0), the left wheel is still red in the `frozenbasis` pseudo-target and render. Freezing only removes the reddish cast over the whole image. Both detached cells render both wheels yellow ([2 × 2 figure](../results/rung0/summary_rung05/figures/examples_bike_29_per_cell.png)).

Pseudo-target hue error on bike agrees with the images:

| | baseline | frozenbasis | detachcurve | detach |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hue error (training views) | 20.7° | 16.0° | 10.8° | 10.7° |

*Inferred:* the flip comes from the curve chasing the render, not from the colour basis.

Caveat (measured): on **validation renders**, the hue metric ranks the cells the other way on bike (detach 31.8° vs baseline 21.5°). The detached renders are much less saturated:
- saturation ratio 0.50 vs 0.77;
- 12% of coloured pixels are excluded for having almost no saturation.

At low saturation the hue is unstable, so on washed-out images this metric mostly measures desaturation. The global angle-error metric doesn't capture the wheel flip either, because the grey background dominates it. The wheel's colour is clear in the images and in the pseudo-target hue error, but no global validation metric reflects it.

**Q6. With a shared reference depth, how do the cells compare on pseudo-target consistency?**

Detaching the curve makes the pseudo-targets less consistent across views. Freezing the basis makes little difference. The table gives pseudo-target relative L1 as a multiple of the ground-truth level. The baseline column leaves out the reference run, so it uses seeds 1 and 2 only.

| | baseline | frozenbasis | detachcurve | detach |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| chair | 0.69 | 0.70 | 1.01 | 0.97 |
| sofa | 1.16 | 1.18 | 1.67 | 1.67 |
| bike | 1.11 | 0.97 | 1.71 | 1.72 |
| buu | 1.29 | 1.30 | 1.40 | 1.56 |
| shrub | 1.27 | 1.24 | 1.09 | 1.19 |

Shrub is the one exception: there `detachcurve` is the most consistent cell. Bike is the only scene where the basis matters. Freezing it improves the ratio from 1.11 to 0.97, and the zero-basis counterfactual of the baseline brings its pseudo-targets from 0.054 to 0.034.

The old own-depth metric had moved a lot. Bike's ground-truth level was 0.075 under baseline depth and 0.043 under detach depth, against 0.049 now for every run. The bike baseline ratio goes from 0.84 to 1.11 under the shared depth ([table 4](../results/rung0/summary_rung05/tables.md)). Rung 0's conclusion, that detaching makes pseudo-targets less consistent, still holds.

**Q7. Which cell is fastest, and why?**

`detach` is fastest:

| Cell | Minutes per 10k-step run | Relative to baseline |
|---|---|---|
| baseline | 12.6 | 1.00 |
| frozenbasis | 13.1 | 1.04 |
| detachcurve | 11.3 | 0.90 |
| detach | 7.9 | 0.63 |

The new runs ran in a later session and got slower towards the end; for example, shrub frozenbasis took 16.2 min against 13.8 for baseline. The smoke tests ran back to back on the same machine, so their per-step ratios compare better: 0.92 (frozenbasis), 0.77 (detachcurve), 0.59 (detach).

*Inferred:* `detach` removes two backward passes, and both cost time:
- the LUT gather, where many pixels add into the same few bins, is the larger cost;
- the per-pixel projection that trains the colour basis is the smaller one.

## Implications for Rung 2

Applying the plan's rules:

- **H3 is not supported.** Freezing the basis explains well under 75% of the brightness-matched detach gain: 10–42% pooled, depending on order, and none on bike. It does not remove the hue flip.
- **Stopping the curve's image gradient explains most of the gain:** 85–100% wherever the gain is real. So **Rung 2 trains its curve from the priors only (detached)**.
  - Known cost: buu, where detaching hurts LPIPS.
  - The detached cells produce noisier, less view-consistent pseudo-targets (Q6) and paler renders (pooled saturation ratio 0.71; bike 0.50).
  - *Inferred:* Rung 2's shared curve and per-view exposure are aimed at exactly these weaknesses, so track saturation and pseudo-target consistency there.
- **The colour-basis question is secondary, and the answer is a fixed RGB basis.** With a detached curve the learned basis barely moves. Removing it never costs anything real: detach vs detachcurve is between −0.63 dB (sofa, 1.6× noise) and +0.65 dB (shrub). It also matches Rung 2's design, which has no per-view colour matrix.
- **Reference baseline for all Rung 2 comparisons: the `detach` cell** (fixed RGB basis, curve trained from the priors only).
  - It is best or tied-best pooled on brightness-matched PSNR (23.25 dB), LPIPS (0.270) and colour angle error (5.15°).
  - It is the fastest cell.
  - Its raw PSNR is lower (18.33 dB pooled) because the priors alone set the brightness (bike renders 0.61 vs ground truth 0.49). Rung 2's exposure model targets that.
- **Keep the official configuration (`baseline`) in every table as "official".** Report the full 2 × 2 as an ablation in the final report.
- Note for the ablation: `frozenbasis` is the best cell on buu and on pooled raw PSNR. A Rung 2 variant that keeps some image gradient on the curve is a plausible later ablation. It is not the default.

## Setup and caveats

- **Code** on branch `rung05-diagnostics`:
  - `--freeze_color_basis`, `--detach_curve_in_target` and `--grad_check` (all default off, so a run with no new flags reproduces the old code);
  - `--rescore_run` and `--ref_depth_run`, with `Diagnostics.rescore()` in `examples/diagnostics.py`;
  - `examples/rescore_runs.py` and `examples/rung05_analyze.py`.
- **Gradient check** (smoke tests, first step). The table matched the plan in all four cells. With both new flags together, the run gave exactly the same validation numbers as `detach`.
- **Re-score validation** on `buu_baseline_seed0`. It reproduced every column of the Rung 0 diagnostics CSVs exactly. Over all 60 runs:
  - validation PSNR matches each run's `stats/val_step9999.json` to below 0.001 dB;
  - the PNG-based brightness-matched PSNR matches Rung 0's values to below 0.001 dB.

  Setting the reference depth to the run itself gives the same output as not setting it.
- **Brightness-matched PSNR** is now computed on float renders. Rung 0 computed it from 8-bit PNGs written by truncation. The switch moves values by a few hundredths of a dB (buu −0.03). The Rung 0 detach deltas reproduce within 0.03 dB: chair +2.15, sofa +2.19, bike +1.87.
- **Shared reference depth:** `<scene>_baseline_seed0` for every run of a scene. That run is excluded from the consistency means, because renders, and attached pseudo-targets, look most consistent under their own geometry.
- **Colour metrics** (all on pixels whose ground truth isn't too dark):
  - `ang_err_deg`: angle between the RGB vectors;
  - `hue_err_sat_deg`: HSV hue difference, only where the ground-truth saturation is above 0.3 and the prediction's above 0.05, with at least 500 such pixels per view;
  - `sat_ratio`: mean saturation of the prediction divided by that of the ground truth.

  The hue metric is unreliable on desaturated predictions (Q5).
- **Qualitative claims** about the wheel rest on seed 0 only. That is the seed the example images were saved for.
- **Changes to the plan, agreed before implementation:**
  - shares reported in both orders;
  - the reference run excluded from consistency means;
  - a PNG-equivalent PSNR kept for validation;
  - a saturation floor on the hue metric;
  - a wider grad check;
  - the zero-basis counterfactual;
  - timing taken from smoke-test ratios;
  - the reports moved to `Luminance-GS/Implementation_guides/`.

  A re-score takes about 9 s, not the minute the plan assumed.
