# Rung 0 results: diagnostics of the Luminance-GS tone curve

Measurement only, no new method. 30 runs on all five LOM scenes (buu, chair, sofa, bike, shrub) × {baseline, detached pseudo-target} × seeds {0, 1, 2}, 10k steps each, with per-view curve snapshots at 9 steps and end-of-training analysis of the pseudo-targets. All runs completed. Tables, CSVs and figures are in [`Luminance-GS/results/rung0/summary/`](../results/rung0/summary/). They are regenerated by `python rung0_analyze.py` in `Luminance-GS/examples`. Numbers below are means over views and seeds unless stated. *Measured* means read directly from the runs; *inferred* marks an interpretation.

## Answers

**Q1. Are the learned curves non-monotonic, and by how much?**
Almost never. At step 10k the per-view curve `curve_adj` has **zero decreasing steps in every view of every run** in buu, chair and sofa, and in bike and shrub with detach. Bike baseline has one decreasing step in a single view, with negligible negative mass. The one substantial exception is **shrub baseline**: every view has about 10 decreasing steps, with a negative mass of 0.013 (≈3.3/255; worst view 0.020 ≈ 5/255). The decreases lie in bins that pixels use and appear after step 3000, when the CDF weight halves ([figure](../results/rung0/summary/figures/q1_negative_mass_all_bins.png)). About 7% of curve entries are clamped, almost all at the top (saturation at 1 above the used input range). The *global* curve on its own is non-monotone above bin ≈220, but those bins are unused and the clamp hides the drop ([figure](../results/rung0/summary/figures/q1_curve_shapes.png)).

**Q2. How many parameters does a per-view curve effectively use? What K gives a monotone fit within 1/255?**
Across views, essentially **one**. In every run the first principal component explains 99.2–99.8% of the cross-view variation, and its score correlates with the view's mean input brightness at **|r| = 0.97–1.00**. That variation is small: the RMS deviation from the scene-mean curve is 1.4–4.9/255. The per-view "bias" network output is large (RMS 51–70/255), but it is almost the same for all views (cross-view std 1.4–4.9/255), so in practice it acts as a second global curve. For one curve, a monotone piecewise-linear fit with **K = 32 segments whose knots cover the used input range** reproduces the used bins to **0.70/255** on average (baseline; 0.38/255 detach). With *uniform* knots, K = 64 still leaves 1.9/255 on the used bins, because all pixels sit in the bottom 14–25% of the curve where it is steepest. Forcing monotonicity costs nothing: monotone and unconstrained fits differ by < 0.1/255 ([figure](../results/rung0/summary/figures/q2_pl_fit_error_vs_K.png)).

**Q3. Do the shape-prior parameters (gamma, alpha, beta) adapt meaningfully?**
Only gamma does. It falls from 1 to a scene mean of **0.07–0.20** between steps 1000 and 3000, then stays flat, with a per-view spread of about 0.0–0.33 (0–2% of views go slightly below 0). **alpha stays at 0.506–0.510 and beta at 1.004–1.010** (init 0.5 and 1), so the s-curve part of the prior is effectively fixed ([figure](../results/rung0/summary/figures/q3_shape_prior.png)). Side effect (measured): `gamma_curve` clamps its input at 1e-3, so with gamma ≈ 0.1 the prior maps the darkest input to 0.30–0.64. The learned curves follow it part of the way. Their output for the darkest input averages 0–0.34 per scene, instead of the HE target's 0 (0.21–0.34 in chair, bike and shrub, consistently across seeds in bike). *Inferred:* this lifted black level is a likely cause of the grey, washed-out look in the bike and shrub renders ([bike](../results/rung0/summary/figures/qual_bike_baseline.png), [shrub](../results/rung0/summary/figures/qual_shrub_baseline.png)).

**Q4. Does detaching the pseudo-target change results beyond seed noise?**
Yes, in 4 of 5 scenes. Raw PSNR alone is misleading here, because detaching also changes overall brightness. In the table, "PSNR, brightness-matched" means PSNR after scaling the render to the ground truth's mean brightness (columns `psnr_gtmean` in [`tables.md`](../results/rung0/summary/tables.md)). Paired detach − baseline, n = 3 seeds; ×noise = mean difference / pooled per-run seed std:

| Scene | Δ PSNR (dB) | Δ PSNR, brightness-matched (dB) | Δ LPIPS |
|---|---|---|---|
| buu | +0.75 (1.9× noise) | +0.35 (1.2×) | +0.016 |
| chair | +1.07 (4.1×) | **+2.16 (15×)** | −0.027 |
| sofa | +0.26 (0.6×) | **+2.16 (5×)** | −0.016 |
| bike | **−2.40 (−7.4×)** | **+1.86 (19×)** | **−0.118** |
| shrub | −0.37 (−0.4×) | −0.31 (−0.4×) | +0.025 |

On bike, the detached renders are brighter than the ground truth (validation mean 0.61 vs 0.49; baseline 0.54). Raw PSNR therefore drops even though brightness-matched PSNR and LPIPS both improve a lot ([baseline](../results/rung0/summary/figures/qual_bike_baseline.png) vs [detach](../results/rung0/summary/figures/qual_bike_detach.png): the baseline turns the yellow wheels red and posterises the pseudo-target). Detached runs are also **about 40% faster** (e.g. buu 12.1 → 7.4 min) at similar Gaussian counts. *Inferred cause:* no backward pass through the LUT gather, where the many atomic adds pile onto the few bins pixels use.

**Q5. What is the real seed-to-seed spread per scene?**
Sample std of validation PSNR over seeds 0–2 (baseline): **buu 0.21, chair 0.37, sofa 0.58, bike 0.45, shrub 1.13 dB**. Brightness-matched PSNR: 0.34, 0.16, 0.46, 0.13, 0.98 dB. This includes GPU nondeterminism: the earlier seed-42 runs already varied by up to 1 dB. The seed-42 means (buu 21.30, chair 17.48, sofa 23.41) lie within about 2 std of the seeds 0–2 means (20.81, 17.76, 23.22) ([figure](../results/rung0/summary/figures/q4_q5_val_metrics.png)).

**Q6. How good are the pseudo-targets against ground truth, and does the 3DGS improve on them?**
Against the normal-light images of the *training* views (oracle; baseline), pseudo-targets score **12.1–25.2 dB raw and 17.8–26.8 dB brightness-matched**. The enhanced render matches or beats its own targets: brightness-matched PSNR is +0.1 to +0.9 dB higher and SSIM is higher in every scene (e.g. buu 0.92 vs 0.87). *Inferred:* fusing many views averages out each target's noise. The one exception is bike with detach, where the render is 1.2 dB below its targets. Brightness is set by the prior, not the scene: pseudo-targets average 0.54–0.62 in every scene, while the ground truth ranges 0.36–0.54. Shrub's 12 dB is mostly over-exposure (0.59 vs 0.36) ([figure](../results/rung0/summary/figures/q6_pseudo_target_quality.png)).

**Q7. How consistent are pseudo-targets across views, compared with real ground-truth images?**
Method: warp each view to its two nearest training cameras using the trained depth, then measure relative L1 (L1 / mean intensity) on visible pixels. With the baseline, pseudo-targets score **0.031–0.145, which is 0.69–1.30× the ground-truth level** (0.028–0.125). They are as consistent as real images in aggregate. **Detaching makes them less consistent: 1.0–2.0× the ground-truth level.** *Inferred:* when attached, the target is pulled toward the render, which is consistent across views by construction. Renders sit at 0.012–0.058, 1.5–4× more consistent than the ground truth. **This is the number Rungs 1 and 2 must improve: pseudo-target relative L1 per scene, against ground truth as the reference** ([figure](../results/rung0/summary/figures/q7_consistency.png)). Caveats: the ground-truth figure also contains warp error from imperfect depth on sharp texture, and the aggregate L1 hides hue inconsistencies such as the bike wheels, which come out red in one view and yellow in the next ([warp check](../results/rung0/summary/figures/warp_check_bike_baseline.png)).

**Also measured:** the histogram-prior terms dominate the loss throughout. On buu, the weighted CDF term is about 0.85 of a total of about 1.25 at the start. At the end, the two histogram terms (0.11) are about 5× all the image terms combined (0.022) ([figure](../results/rung0/summary/figures/loss_components.png)). The curve is shaped mainly by the priors, not by the images.

## Implications for Rung 1 and Rung 2

- **Monotonicity on its own will change little.** The curves are already monotone except shrub baseline (≤ 5/255). Rung 1's value has to come from compactness, interpolation and anchoring. All pixels fall in 14–25% of the 255 bins, so most of the LUT never receives gradient from the images. A curve fixed at F(0) = 0 would also remove the lifted black level (Q3; the causal link is inferred).
- **Rung 2 is the most strongly supported rung.** The cross-view curve variation is one-dimensional and tracks input brightness at |r| ≥ 0.97. A shared monotone curve plus one exposure value per view, `F(min(1, 2^{s_k} x))`, matches that structure directly. *Inferred:* the 255-dim per-view bias network can be replaced by a scalar per view without losing anything it currently expresses.
- **Starting K for Rung 1: 32**, with knots concentrated in the dark input range: spread over the used range, or log- or quantile-spaced. Uniform knots would need more than 64 segments on the bins that matter.
- **The shape prior is effectively a one-parameter gamma curve.** alpha and beta do not move. Replacing it with a structured curve loses nothing measurable. Avoid clamping input at 1e-3 under small gamma.
- **Detaching matters, so run Rungs 1–3 in both modes.** Report raw and brightness-matched PSNR side by side, since detaching shifts global brightness. Detaching is also 40% cheaper per run.
- **Smallest reliably detectable effect** with 3 seeds per arm (difference > 2 standard errors ≈ 1.6× the per-run std): raw PSNR about **0.3 (buu), 0.6 (chair), 0.9 (sofa), 0.7 (bike), 1.8 dB (shrub)**; brightness-matched about 0.2–0.7 dB, 1.6 dB for shrub. Use at least 3 seeds and the brightness-matched metric. Raw PSNR on shrub and bike is dominated by global brightness, which the HE prior fixes at about 0.55–0.6 whatever the scene.

## Setup and caveats

- Code on branch `rung0-diagnostics`: config flags `--seed`, `--detach_pseudo_target`, `--diagnostics`, `--diag_steps`; the shared helper `Runner.curve_forward`; `examples/diagnostics.py`; `examples/run_rung0.py`; `examples/rung0_analyze.py`. A run with no new flags reproduces the original code. The helper's outputs are bit-identical to the original, and over 200 steps the training image order was identical with and without `--diagnostics`, with loss differences below the run-to-run noise floor.
- The COLMAP cameras are not pinhole, so training images are undistorted and cropped to 374×499. The ground-truth images for training views receive the same undistortion before scoring.
- "Brightness-matched" (exposure-aligned, the GT-mean protocol) means the prediction is scaled by mean(GT) / mean(prediction) over all pixels and channels, then clamped to [0, 1].
- The scores against training-view ground truth are oracle diagnostics; the ground truth was never used in training.
- The PC slept for about 11 h during `sofa_detach_seed1`. Its results are unaffected, but its wall time is excluded from the timing figures.
- Differences from the plan's description of the code: `LUT_mapping` truncates rather than rounds; the smoothness term is squared differences, not TV; the pseudo-curve loss also includes `0.01·MSE(pseudo, CDF)`; the HE-CDF is computed on a downsampled grey image while the LUT acts on the colour channels scaled to [0, 1]; and a diagnostics `DataLoader` would have consumed random numbers and changed the training order, so views are read directly instead.
